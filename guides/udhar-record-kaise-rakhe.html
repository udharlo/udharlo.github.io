Shopkeeper Bookkeeping Guide

How to Keep Udhar Records for Your Shop (Paper or App)

Udhar (customer credit) is the lifeline of local Indian retail, but unorganized bookkeeping leads to disputes, forgotten dues, and locked working capital. Here is how to keep an accurate, dispute-free khata register.

Author: Suresh Vaishnav Published: October 2026 Reading time: 6 mins

1. The Essential Fields Every Udhar Entry Must Have

Whether you use a paper bahi khata register or an Android khata app, missing a single field can result in a dispute when asking for settlement. Every transaction line must record:

  • Date and Time: Exactly when the goods were taken or payment was received.
  • Customer Name & Mobile Number: Never record just "Sharma Ji" or "Chintu's Dad". Use their full name and phone number to avoid confusion with similar names.
  • Item Description / Bill No.: Brief description of items (e.g., "5kg Aata + Oil" or "Bill #104").
  • Credit Given (उधार / Diya): The exact rupee value of goods handed over without immediate payment.
  • Payment Received (जमा / Liya): The exact amount collected, along with the mode (Cash, UPI, PhonePe, GPay).
  • Running Balance (बाकी / Net Dues): The updated total amount the customer owes immediately following this entry.

Notice how the running balance is updated on every single row. If you only total at the end of the month, errors creep in and customers question older items.

3. Daily vs. Weekly Settling Routines for Counter Stores

A ledger is only as good as the discipline behind it. Establish these two routines:

The 10-Minute Daily Close (शाम का हिसाब)

  1. Match loose slips: If you wrote entries on rough counter slips during busy afternoon hours, transfer them to the customer ledgers before pulling down the shop shutter.
  2. Verify UPI receipts: Check that payment notifications received on your Soundbox or phone were credited to the correct customer's Jama column.
  3. Check Today's Dues: Note which regular customers had promised payment for today.

The Sunday Weekly Reconciliation

Every Sunday or month-end, review balances older than 30 days. Categorize them into:

  • Active regular credit (0 to 15 days): Healthy customers who buy weekly and clear dues on salary day.
  • Ageing credit (15 to 30 days): Requires a polite, friendly WhatsApp nudge with balance.
  • Overdue credit (30+ days): Stop granting further credit until the old balance is cleared.

4. The 3 Costly Mistakes That Cause Customer Disputes

In retail credit, 90% of disputes are not due to dishonest customers; they happen because memory fades over time. Avoid these 3 mistakes:

Mistake #1: Not writing the entry immediately

When the counter is crowded, it is tempting to tell yourself "I'll write this ₹350 milk entry later". By evening, you either forget who took it or guess the wrong amount. If you are busy, use Voice Entry on an app like Udhar Lo to speak the entry hands-free.

Mistake #2: Not calculating the running balance

If you only write "+250", "+400", "-500" without updating the total, neither you nor the customer knows the exact pending balance. When you ask for ₹1,800 a month later, the customer feels surprised. A running balance ensures zero ambiguity.

Mistake #3: No customer-side record

When the shopkeeper is the only person who holds the record, the customer inherently doubts older transactions ("I didn't take that on the 12th!"). Sending a one-tap WhatsApp summary or PDF ledger receipt after major entries creates mutual trust and documented proof.

5. When to Stop Giving Udhar: 4 Golden Rules for Shop Owners

Udhar should help increase sales, not destroy your cash flow. Keep these strict rules:

  1. The 30-Day Hard Cap: No customer should carry forward an unpaid balance past their agreed salary or monthly cycle without at least a 50% partial payment.
  2. Credit Limits per Customer: Assign a credit limit based on their purchase history (e.g., ₹2,000 for regular neighbors, ₹500 for newer buyers). Once reached, politely request payment before adding more items.
  3. Never give udhar on high-margin third-party items: Avoid giving credit on cigarettes, mobile recharge, or third-party gift cards where your profit margin is tiny (1-2%) and a default wipes out weeks of profit.
  4. No udhar to strangers: Never extend credit to first-time shoppers or people passing through town without a verified reference.
